\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background.} Fisher--Kolmogorov--Petrovskii--Piskunov (Fisher--KPP) models describe glioma spread and growth but do not explicitly represent oxygen limitation or necrotic material. We developed a minimal coupled extension and an interactive workflow to examine these assumptions.\par
\noindent\textbf{Methods.} Tissue-dependent Fisher--KPP growth was compared with oxygen-limited growth and a three-field viable-tumor--oxygen--necrosis model on the BrainWeb normal phantom over 90 days, using assigned biological parameters. Analytical limits, conservation checks, ablations and refinement assessed the numerical method. A separate homogeneous 3D experiment recovered diffusion and proliferation from noisy, blurred, voxel-averaged synthetic scans. Early scans calibrated the model; later scans were withheld. Confidence intervals used local sensitivities and \RecoveryBootstraps{} bootstrap fits, with \RecoveryTrials{} independent noise realizations auditing local-interval coverage.\par
\noindent\textbf{Results.} On the 2 mm BrainWeb grid, the coupled model contained \CoupledCells{} billion viable cells at day 90, versus \ReferenceCells{} billion for Fisher--KPP, a \CellReduction\% reduction; low-threshold volumes differed by \VolumeReduction\%. Growth inhibition dominated this difference. Necrotic material totaled \NecroticCells{} million cell equivalents, but its peak and minimum oxygen remained grid sensitive. In the separate recovery experiment, known $D=\RecoveryDTrue$ mm$^2$/day and $\rho=\RecoveryRhoTrue$ day$^{-1}$ were estimated as \RecoveryDEstimate{} and \RecoveryRhoEstimate{}, respectively; both lay within bootstrap 95\% intervals. Local intervals covered the known rates in \RecoveryDCoverageCount/\RecoveryTrials{} and \RecoveryRhoCoverageCount/\RecoveryTrials{} trials. Day-90 noise-free signal RMSE was \RecoveryDayNinetyRMSE{}.\par
\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.} Oxygen feedback can change the modeled interior while preserving similar threshold-defined extent. Two-rate recovery is feasible under known initialization and imaging assumptions, but does not validate patient-level estimation or the oxygen--necrosis mechanisms. Translation requires calibrated observation operators, broader uncertainty assessment, independent biological targets and external forecasts.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} glioma; reaction--diffusion; oxygen; necrosis; BrainWeb; parameter identifiability; MRI; validation.

The manuscript, source code, interactive Jupyter notebooks, recorded simulations, synthetic recovery observations and synchronized visualizations are available at \url{https://github.com/arvidl/brainweb_fisher_kpp}. The manuscript project includes LaTeX/BibTeX sources, simulation and figure-generation scripts, machine-readable experiment records, source and data hashes, and numerical-test results. An immutable release identifier and archival DOI should accompany a submitted version when available. BrainWeb attribution and source records are retained separately from the software.
\subsection*{Study scope and declarations}
All experiments are computer simulations on anatomical or homogeneous phantoms. No patient measurements, new human sampling or new animal experiments were used. Parameter recovery used synthetic measurements only; patient calibration and external biological validation remain proposed work.
