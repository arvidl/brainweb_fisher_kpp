\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Background.} Fisher--Kolmogorov--Petrovskii--Piskunov (Fisher--KPP) models describe glioma spread and growth but do not explicitly represent oxygen limitation or necrotic material. We developed a minimal coupled extension and an interactive workflow to examine these assumptions.\par
\noindent\textbf{Methods.} Tissue-dependent cell diffusion and logistic growth were compared with oxygen-limited growth and a three-field model coupling viable tumor, quasi-steady relative oxygen and immobile necrotic cell equivalents. Simulations used the BrainWeb normal phantom, an identical dense Gaussian seed and assigned biological parameters over 90 days. Conservative finite-volume transport, operator splitting and an elliptic oxygen solver were assessed using analytical limits, conservation checks, ablations and refinement. Measurement-based inference and independent validation were formulated as future protocols.\par
\noindent\textbf{Results.} On the 2 mm grid, the coupled model contained \CoupledCells{} billion viable cells at day 90, compared with \ReferenceCells{} billion for Fisher--KPP, a \CellReduction\% reduction. Volumes above 0.2 of the density scale differed by \VolumeReduction\%. Minimum relative oxygen was \MinimumOxygen{}, and necrotic material totaled \NecroticCells{} million cell equivalents. Only three voxels exceeded the necrotic threshold of 0.2. Suppressing hypoxic death had a small effect on total viable burden, whereas removing burden-dependent supply suppression largely restored the reference burden. Time refinement reduced field errors, while the necrotic peak and minimum oxygen remained sensitive to spatial resolution.\par
\noindent\textbf{Conclusions.} Oxygen feedback can change the simulated interior substantially while producing similar threshold-defined extent. This is a reproducible computational comparison, not patient-specific parameter estimation or biological validation. Translation requires calibrated observation operators, identifiable parameter subsets, independent spatial measurements and withheld-data forecasts.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} glioma; reaction--diffusion; oxygen; necrosis; BrainWeb; parameter identifiability; MRI; validation.

\subsection*{Code, data and supplementary material}
The manuscript, source code, interactive Jupyter notebooks, recorded simulations and synchronized visualizations are available at \url{https://github.com/arvidl/brainweb_fisher_kpp}. The manuscript project includes LaTeX/BibTeX sources, simulation and figure-generation scripts, machine-readable experiment records, source and data hashes, and numerical-test results. Repository history and the recorded source hashes identify the implementation used for this study. An immutable release identifier and archival DOI should accompany a submitted version when available. BrainWeb attribution and source records are retained separately from the software.
\subsection*{Study scope and declarations}
All experiments are computer simulations on an anatomical phantom. No patient measurements, new human sampling or new animal experiments were used. Biological parameter inference and external validation remain proposed work.
