\section{Numerical implementation and verification}\label{app:numerics}
\subsection{Conservative transport and reaction splitting}
For neighboring active cells $i,j$ on a Cartesian grid of spacing $h$, use the harmonic face coefficient
\begin{equation}
 D_{ij}=\begin{cases}2D_iD_j/(D_i+D_j),&D_i+D_j>0,\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad
 w_{ij}=D_{ij}/h^2 .
\end{equation}
Faces leaving the active domain are assigned zero weight, as are external volume faces. A cell-transport step is
\begin{equation}
 c_i^*=c_i^n+\Delta t\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}w_{ij}(c_j^n-c_i^n),\qquad
 \Delta t\leq\frac{0.9}{\max_i\sum_j w_{ij}} .
\end{equation}
Symmetric fluxes conserve total cell count under pure diffusion. The step bound is sufficient for nonnegative explicit updates. Faces are stored without periodic wrapping; no density clipping is applied. Requested steps are subdivided as needed, and steps end at the exact saved output times.

For M0, the reaction following diffusion is the exact logistic flow
\begin{equation}
 c_i^{n+1}=\frac{c_i^*}{\exp(-\rho_i\Delta t)+(c_i^*/K_i)[1-\exp(-\rho_i\Delta t)]}.
\end{equation}
For M2, freeze oxygen at $o^n$ and necrosis at $z^n$ during birth. Set $F_i=K_i-z_i^n$. If $F_i>0$ and $c_i^*<F_i$, use
\begin{equation}
 r_i=\rho_i g(o_i^n)F_i/K_i,\qquad
 c_i^b=\frac{c_i^*}{\exp(-r_i\Delta t)+(c_i^*/F_i)[1-\exp(-r_i\Delta t)]}.
\end{equation}
Otherwise $c_i^b=c_i^*$. With $q_i=\delta h(o_i^n)$, the subsequent death/clearance step is
\begin{equation}
 L_i=c_i^b[1-\exp(-q_i\Delta t)],\quad
 c_i^{n+1}=c_i^b-L_i,\quad
 z_i^{n+1}=(z_i^n+L_i)\exp(-\lambda_z\Delta t).
\end{equation}
The same $L_i$ leaves $c$ and enters $z$, preserving cell equivalents when clearance is zero. Exact frozen-coefficient substeps do not make the full coupled update exact: diffusion, birth, death and clearance are Lie-split, and oxygen is lagged. The complete method is first order in time. The centered spatial stencil has second-order consistency in smooth regions, without a global spatial-order claim at sharp tissue boundaries. The BrainWeb tumor and necrosis arrays use float32 and compiled Numba CPU kernels; both synthetic inference wrappers use float64 for stable finite-difference sensitivities.

\subsection{Quasi-steady oxygen solve}
Let $L_h$ be the unit-diffusivity no-flux Laplacian on the active voxel graph, $u=c/K$, and $p_i=[1+\alpha(c_i+z_i)/K_i]^{-1}$. After initialization and each tumor update, solve
\begin{equation}
 \left[-\ell_o^2L_h+\operatorname{diag}(p+\beta u)\right]\bm{o}=\bm{p}.
\end{equation}
The positive reaction diagonal makes this system symmetric positive definite, including disconnected tissue components. Matrix-free conjugate gradients use float64 arithmetic, the previous oxygen state as the initial guess and a Jacobi preconditioner. The relative residual tolerance is $10^{-7}$, with a 200-iteration maximum per solve. Nonconvergence or oxygen-bound failure raises an error. The zero-uptake and zero-diffusion limits have direct solutions. Stored oxygen is float32. Outside-domain zeros are excluded-domain sentinels and are excluded from oxygen statistics and overlays.

The primary M2 run required 361 solves and 873 total conjugate-gradient iterations; the largest recorded relative residual was below $10^{-7}$. This residual measures algebraic solution accuracy, not accuracy of the continuum approximation on the chosen grid.

\section{Synthetic recovery: observation, optimization and resampling}\label{app:recovery}
\subsection{Imaging operator and fixed design}
The homogeneous box is $[-30,30]^3$ mm with reflecting boundaries. For known $u_0$, the initial cell averages are computed from the product of one-dimensional Gaussian integrals using the error function. The fine generator has $60^3$ cells and inversion has $30^3$ cells. Both map to $10^3$ observation voxels per scan. For a Gaussian point-spread kernel $G_{\sigma_b}$ with $\sigma_b=2$ mm and an observation voxel $V_v$ of volume $6^3$ mm$^3$,
\begin{equation}
 H_v[u(t)]=\frac{1}{|V_v|}\int_{V_v}(G_{\sigma_b}*u)(\bm x,t)\,\dd\bm x,\qquad
 y_{jv}=H_v[u(t_j)]+\epsilon_{jv},\quad
 \epsilon_{jv}\sim\mathcal N(0,0.03^2).
\end{equation}
Implementation uses a discrete Gaussian filter with reflecting extension, followed by aligned block averages. The same signal calibration and blur are assumed known in both models. Noise is independent across observation voxels and scan times and is added after averaging; blurring independently generated noise instead would require a correlated likelihood. Values outside $[0,1]$ are retained. All training voxels are used without a data-dependent region selection. The cellularity proxy has a known normalized scale and does not identify an absolute $K$ from imaging.

\subsection{Maximum likelihood and local information}
With $\theta=(\log D,\log\rho)$, using numerical values in mm$^2$/day and day$^{-1}$, define
\begin{equation}
 r_{jv}(\theta)=\frac{H_v[u(e^{\theta_1},e^{\theta_2},t_j)]-y_{jv}}{0.03},\qquad
 \widehat\theta=\arg\min_\theta\tfrac12\|\bm r(\theta)\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
The bounds are $0.05\leq D\leq0.8$ mm$^2$/day and $0.01\leq\rho\leq0.09$ day$^{-1}$; they are optimizer constraints, not a prior distribution. Starting pairs are $(0.12,0.02)$, $(0.30,0.04)$ and $(0.65,0.075)$. Bounded trust-region least squares uses a three-point finite-difference Jacobian with relative step $10^{-4}$ and Jacobian column scaling \citep{virtanen2020}. Termination tolerances are $10^{-8}$ for objective/step changes and $10^{-6}$ for the gradient. The selected fit minimizes training residual sum of squares. Inversion has a fixed maximum time step that is stable throughout the bounds; it does not change with the fitted rate except for landing at exact output times.

Let $J=\partial\bm r/\partial\theta$ at the fit. The singular-value decomposition gives
\begin{equation}
 J=USV^{\mathsf T},\qquad C_\theta=VS^{-2}V^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 I_i=\exp\left[\widehat\theta_i\pm1.95996\sqrt{(C_\theta)_{ii}}\right].
\end{equation}
Because measurement variance is known, $C_\theta$ is not rescaled by fitted residual variance. The smallest singular value must exceed $10^{-8}$ times the largest. A fit within $10^{-4}$ log units of a bound is flagged and its Wald intervals must not be interpreted as calibrated. In the primary experiment, weighted singular values were approximately 107.9 and 18.0. With only $t=0$, both rate sensitivities vanish, and the fit is rejected as locally unidentifiable.

\subsection{Bootstrap, forecasts and repeated-noise audit}
For each of \RecoveryBootstraps{} bootstrap trials, training observations were generated from the fitted coarse-grid mean plus new Gaussian noise and refitted from the primary estimate \citep{efron1979}. Marginal percentile intervals use the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. Every fitted parameter pair was propagated to the five scan times. Pointwise mean-signal bands use trajectory percentiles. Approximate new-scan prediction bands resample these bootstrap trajectories 1,000 times and add independent observation noise before taking percentiles. These constructions are conditional frequentist approximations, not samples from a Bayesian posterior, and do not provide simultaneous spatial coverage.

The separate coverage audit generated \RecoveryTrials{} noisy training datasets from the fine-grid signal at the known parameters. Each fit started from $(0.25,0.04)$. Bias is the mean estimate minus the known value; RMSE is the square root of mean squared estimation error. Coverage counts intervals containing the known rate. Exact two-sided binomial 95\% intervals quantify sampling uncertainty in these fractions. The audit concerns local Jacobian intervals; bootstrap coverage would require a separate repeated or nested study. All optimization failures or boundary fits stop execution rather than being discarded. There were none in the recorded experiment.

NumPy seed 20261017 spawns four independent streams for the primary noise, bootstrap, repeated-noise audit and predictive noise. The known truth is used to evaluate recovery and forecasts, not to select fitted parameters or tune uncertainty. Unknown age, seed geometry, noise variance, capacity, registration, observation calibration and model discrepancy are not inferred. The mesh/time comparison uses the same PDE family and is a numerical sensitivity check, not validation against a different biological mechanism.


\section{BrainWeb recovery: tissue rates and anatomy sensitivity}\label{app:bw_recovery}
\subsection{Domain, seed and physical observation operator}
The full 3 mm BrainWeb volume has the affine translation of Appendix~\ref{app:data} and diagonal spacing $(3,3,3)$ mm. The active set contains labels 2, 3 and 8; its 58,669 voxels evolve using the conservative harmonic-face kernel with float64 density. Outside-domain values remain zero, and no cells cross CSF or extracerebral boundaries. The normalized model is
\begin{equation}
 \partial_t u=\nabla\!\cdot(D(\bm x)\nabla u)+\rho u(1-u),\qquad
 D(\bm x)=\begin{cases}D_{\rm WM},&\text{label 3},\\D_{\rm GM},&\text{labels 2 or 8},\end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $u_0(\bm x)=0.2\exp[-\frac12\sum_k((x_k-x_{0k})/6)^2]\mathbf 1_\Omega$, where $\bm x_0=(-30,25,30)$ mm. The analytic Gaussian is point-sampled and untruncated; it differs from both the integrated homogeneous initialization and the dense, truncated anatomical forward seed. Its absolute cell scale is known only by assumption; $K$ is not estimated.

Observation centers along each axis are
\begin{equation}
 x_{v,k}=a_k+(v+\tfrac12-5)6\ \mathrm{mm},\qquad v=0,\ldots,9,\qquad \bm a=(-30,24,30)\ \mathrm{mm}.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal I_h$ be trilinear interpolation in RAS coordinates and $G_{2,h}$ the discrete 2 mm Gaussian filter. Approximate voxel averaging uses the fixed midpoint offsets $s_l\in\{-2,0,2\}$ mm:
\begin{equation}
 H_v^h[u]=\frac1{27}\sum_{l_1,l_2,l_3=1}^3
 \mathcal I_h[G_{2,h}u](\bm x_v+(s_{l_1},s_{l_2},s_{l_3})),\qquad
 y_{jv}=H_v^h[u(t_j)]+\epsilon_{jv},\quad\epsilon_{jv}\sim\mathcal N(0,0.02^2).
\end{equation}
The filter uses zero extension outside the image, with the tumor field zero outside parenchyma. Noise is independent after imaging and is never clipped. Physical locations and quadrature remain fixed across 3 mm and 2 mm grids. This is a calibrated normalized-cellularity proxy; T1 intensity is not the tumor observation. No fitted or true-signal threshold selects the FOV or near-seed evaluation region.

\subsection{Three-rate likelihood, uncertainty and controls}
The log-rate vector is $\theta=(\log D_{\rm WM},\log D_{\rm GM},\log\rho)$, with numerical rates in the stated units. Minimize the squared residuals $(H_v^h[u(t_j)]-y_{jv})/0.02$ at days 20, 40 and 60. Search bounds are $(0.05,0.02,0.01)$ to $(1.0,0.6,0.09)$, and starts are $(0.25,0.20,0.025)$, $(0.45,0.12,0.04)$ and $(0.80,0.05,0.065)$. No tissue-ratio equality, ordering or probability prior is imposed. The finite-difference settings, log-covariance construction, singular-value rank criterion and boundary flag follow Appendix~\ref{app:recovery}, now with three columns. A sufficient diffusion bound is $6\max(D_{\rm WM},D_{\rm GM})\Delta t/h^2\leq0.9$, satisfied throughout the search region. Steps land exactly at scan times.

The primary generator uses the same 3 mm geometry with a 0.125-day maximum step; inversion uses 0.5 day. Bootstrap training samples are drawn from the fitted inverse mean and refitted from its estimate. The \BWTrials{} independent coverage trials instead use the fine-time-step known-rate mean, each starting from $(0.4,0.15,0.035)$. No failed or boundary trial is omitted; either stops the notebook. Time-zero data are rejected because all three sensitivities vanish. Missing-tissue rank deficiency is also tested in the software suite.

NumPy seed 20261018 spawns independent primary, bootstrap, coverage and predictive-noise streams. Each of \BWBootstraps{} fitted bootstrap triples is propagated to all five scan times; resampling these trajectories 1,000 times and adding new independent noise gives approximate pointwise new-scan prediction intervals. The withheld days do not enter any fit. Whole-FOV metrics use 1,000 voxels, while near-seed metrics use the 120 voxel centers within a prespecified 18 mm radius of $\bm x_0$. These spatial fractions should not be read as independent coverage audits.

\subsection{Separate anatomy/grid perturbation}
The sensitivity generator uses the full $91\times109\times91$ BrainWeb volume at 2 mm and a 0.125-day step, keeping physical rates, analytic seed definition, imaging settings and observation locations fixed. The 3 mm inverse model fits its noise-free mean and a paired noisy dataset with exactly the primary noise draw added. The noiseless residual objective uses the same constant noise weight, which only rescales the objective; no sampling interval is interpreted for that deterministic fit. Both fits begin at $(0.4,0.15,0.035)$.

Nearest-neighbor tissue resampling changes the interface graph. Point sampling of the Gaussian and the discrete blur/interpolation also change, so the fitted shifts combine anatomy and numerical representation. The test is neither a pure mesh-convergence experiment nor an empirical distribution of segmentation errors. Halving the inverse time step on the fixed 3 mm anatomy is reported separately. No patient, oxygen or necrosis parameter is inferred in these experiments.

\section{Inverse formulation, priors and identifiability}\label{app:inference}
\subsection{An example estimation objective}
With the observation model in Equation~\eqref{eq:observation}, a possible maximum-a-posteriori objective for continuous approximately Gaussian observations is
\begin{align}
 (\widehat{\bm\theta},\widehat{\bm\eta})
 &=\arg\min_{\bm\theta,\bm\eta}\ \mathcal J(\bm\theta,\bm\eta),\\
 \mathcal J&=\frac12\sum_{m,j}\bm r_{mj}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{mj}^{-1}\bm r_{mj}
 -\log\pi(\bm\theta,\bm\eta),\qquad
 \bm r_{mj}=\mathcal H_m[\bm s_{\bm\theta}(t_j);\bm\eta_m]-\bm y_{mj},
\end{align}
where $\bm s=(c,o,z)$ and $\Sigma$ models measurement covariance. Cross-time or cross-modality correlations require a joint likelihood rather than independent terms. Segmentations may need a probabilistic classification likelihood rather than Gaussian residuals. Registration, partial volume, scanner bias, initial fields and uncertain state-to-signal mappings should enter the likelihood or be propagated as nuisance uncertainty. Bayesian sampling can quantify uncertainty; an optimizer alone does not provide calibrated predictive intervals. No coupled-model or patient-data maximum-a-posteriori fit was performed; the synthetic analyses in Appendices~\ref{app:recovery} and~\ref{app:bw_recovery} used maximum likelihood without a prior.

\subsection{Important structural and practical ambiguities}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Absolute cell scale.} In terms of $u=c/K$ and $w=z/K$ with uniform $K$, the normalized dynamics do not determine $K$. Scaling $(c,z,K)$ together leaves $(u,w,o)$ unchanged. An observation dependent only on normalized density cannot identify the absolute cell count without external calibration.
\item \textbf{Age and rates.} For an unknown elapsed tumor age, multiplying $D$, $\rho$, $\delta$ and $\lambda_z$ by a common factor and dividing the elapsed time by that factor leaves the autonomous state trajectory at the observation unchanged. Known longitudinal time intervals can break this particular scaling, although seed and parameter confounding may remain.
\item \textbf{Oxygen-growth coupling.} Where all observed cells remain below $\theta_p$, the birth term depends on $\rho/\theta_p$ rather than on those parameters separately, for a fixed oxygen field. Oxygen threshold calibration and observations spanning informative regimes are needed.
\item \textbf{Delivery and uptake.} Neglecting spatial oxygen transport locally gives
\begin{equation}
 o=\frac{1}{1+\beta u[1+\alpha(u+w)]}.
\end{equation}
One local state constrains an effective supply--uptake combination, not separate mechanisms. Spatially varying, calibrated oxygen observations across burdens could help, but do not guarantee recovery. An exchange length far below measurement and mesh resolution can be practically unidentifiable.
\item \textbf{Quasi-steady reduction.} If a dimensional oxygen equation is divided by a baseline restoration rate, $\ell_o^2$ and $\beta$ represent coefficient ratios. Steady observations alone cannot identify an independent absolute oxygen relaxation time removed by that reduction.
\item \textbf{Necrotic history.} For known $q=\delta h(o)$,
\begin{equation}
 z(t)=z(0)e^{-\lambda_z t}+\int_0^t q(s)c(s)e^{-\lambda_z(t-s)}\,\dd s .
\end{equation}
A single terminal necrotic measurement cannot generally separate formation history and clearance. Zero clearance is a model choice here, not an estimated absence of biological removal.
\end{enumerate}

Positive parameters can be transformed as $\log\theta_k\sim\mathcal N(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)$, with dimensional reference units stated. For thresholds, use $\theta_p=\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(a)$ and $\theta_d=\theta_p\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(b)$. A zero-clearance component or other exact-zero ablation requires an explicit point mass or a separate model, rather than a continuous positive prior. Neither prior hyperparameters nor population distributions are supplied by the current phantom experiment.

Before a full fit, evaluate scaled observation sensitivities, their singular values, profile likelihoods and posterior dependence across plausible initial states and observation mappings. The number of voxels is not the number of independent constraints. Begin with a global migration scale and growth coefficient, restrict other quantities using external information, and add freedom only when independent observations support it. Identifiability and predictive comparison should be repeated for each candidate model and intended measurement set.
